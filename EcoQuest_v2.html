What to know for the test

4.1

Energy, Producers, and Consumers

▼
  • Differentiate between autotrophs and heterotrophs.
  • Compare photosynthesis and chemosynthesis.
  • Identify different types of consumers: herbivores, carnivores, omnivores, scavengers, detritivores, and decomposers.
  • Explain the difference between decomposers and detritivores and their roles in recycling matter.
4.2

Energy Flow in Ecosystems

▼
  • Differentiate between food chains and food webs.
  • Identify organisms at different trophic levels within a food web.
  • Interpret the direction of arrows in a food web.
  • Predict how changes in one population affect other populations within a food web.
  • Interpret the three ecological pyramids: energy, biomass, and numbers.
  • Apply the 10% rule to calculate energy available at different trophic levels.
  • Explain what happens to energy and matter that are not transferred to the next trophic level.
  • Explain why matter cycles while energy flows through an ecosystem.
4.3

Cycles of Matter

▼
  • Explain how matter is conserved and recycled through biogeochemical cycles.
  • Carbon Cycle: Explain how carbon moves through photosynthesis, cellular respiration, consumption, and decomposition.
  • Nitrogen Cycle: Explain nitrogen fixation, denitrification, and the role of bacteria in making nitrogen available to organisms.
  • Phosphorus Cycle: Explain how phosphorus enters ecosystems and how its cycle differs from the other cycles.
  • Explain how photosynthesis and cellular respiration transform energy and cycle matter.
  • Predict how changes in biological processes can affect atmospheric CO₂ levels.
7.2

Human Impacts on Ecosystems

▼
  • Explain the causes and consequences of eutrophication.
  • Explain how increasing atmospheric CO₂ contributes to ocean acidification.
  • Differentiate between bioaccumulation and biomagnification.
  • Explain why organisms at higher trophic levels may have higher concentrations of pollutants.
  • Connect human activities to disruptions in biogeochemical cycles and food webs.

Skills to Practice

▼
  • Interpreting scientific diagrams and models.
  • Applying concepts to unfamiliar ecosystems and real-world scenarios.
  • Identifying and correcting scientific misconceptions.
  • Evaluating scientific claims using biological reasoning.
  • Writing explanations that connect multiple concepts, particularly matter cycling and energy flow.
